## Adversarial edit decisions

The original spent too much time explaining mechanics and moving between panels. Cut the on-screen reset, the second client graph selection, the pre-approval context modal, detailed SQLite enumeration, and spoken discussion of unfinished integrations. Keep the single post-approval manifest because it proves the permission change reached the worker. A text caption can identify the demo as “Synthetic company data · prototype.” The accurate one-process sentence makes a long security disclaimer unnecessary.

The two profile checks are not agent conversations. Keep “permission profiles” and “real policy check” in the voiceover. The graph depicts recorded inputs, not every theoretical permission. Avoid “unhackable,” “zero leakage,” “production secure,” and “isolated VMs.”

Four primary proof moments, in order: same document allowed/denied; exact approval consumed with C2 unchanged; actual calculator recalculation; inline public-delivery refusal. The client-brief comparison is a short supporting proof that higher clearance does not bypass client scope.

Capture real transitions before voiceover assembly. Shorten model waiting with a small “Generation time shortened” caption. Do not animate a success that did not occur. If the five-minute approval expires, record a fresh authorized mission. Generate the narrator audio separately from live app sounds so cuts stay clean.

For the voice service, use only an approved voice. Suggested pronunciation: “Hydra D B,” “C two,” “chief of staff,” and “ten percent.” Have Bill confirm the pronunciation of Trevillyan before generating the full track, or use his established pronunciation dictionary if available. Do not invent a phonetic spelling of his name.
